About Unix timestamps

A Unix timestamp counts the seconds since midnight UTC on 1 January 1970, a moment called the Unix epoch. Computers store time this way because it is one plain number that means the same moment everywhere in the world. For example, 1767225600 is midnight UTC on 1 January 2026.

Seconds or milliseconds?

A 10-digit timestamp is usually in seconds. A 13-digit one is in milliseconds, which JavaScript and many web APIs use. If you leave the unit on "Detect automatically", this page works out which one you have.

What is ISO 8601?

A standard way to write a date and time as text, such as 2026-12-25T09:00:00+10:00: the date, a T, the time, then the offset from UTC. A Z at the end means UTC. Dates written this way also sort correctly as plain text.

Time zones in Australia

Queensland, New South Wales, Victoria, Tasmania and the ACT use UTC+10. South Australia and the Northern Territory use UTC+9:30, and Western Australia uses UTC+8. During daylight saving, New South Wales, Victoria, Tasmania and the ACT move to UTC+11 and South Australia to UTC+10:30. To avoid daylight saving bugs, store times in UTC and convert them only when you show them. To compare Australian and Indian time, try our India ⇄ Australia time checker.

What is the year 2038 problem?

Older systems that store Unix time as a signed 32-bit number run out of room at 03:14:07 UTC on 19 January 2038. Modern systems use 64-bit numbers, which last far longer.
