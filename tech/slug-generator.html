Accents are removed (é becomes e), "&" becomes "and", and punctuation is dropped. Letters from other scripts are kept. Max length 0 means no limit; longer slugs are cut at a whole word.

What makes a good URL slug?

A slug is the readable part of a web address that names a page, such as australian-resume-cover-letter-guide. Google's URL structure guidelines recommend simple, descriptive words in addresses, with hyphens between them, because people (and search engines) can tell what the page is about before they open it.

For example, the title "How to Get AHPRA Registration as a Nurse & Pass the OSCE!" becomes how-to-get-ahpra-registration-as-a-nurse-and-pass-the-osce. With "Drop small words" ticked, it becomes the shorter how-get-ahpra-registration-nurse-pass-osce.

Tips for slugs

  • Use hyphens, not underscores - search engines read a hyphen as a space between words.
  • Keep slugs short and lower case, and include the main words people search for.
  • Leave out dates and numbers that will go out of date, unless the page is about that year.
  • Once a page is published, avoid changing its slug. If you must, set up a redirect from the old address so links and search rankings are not lost.

What about Malayalam and other scripts?

This tool keeps letters from other scripts, and modern browsers show them correctly. But when the link is copied or shared, each letter is turned into long percent codes (see the URL encoder), so many bilingual sites use English words in the slug and keep Malayalam in the page title and text.
