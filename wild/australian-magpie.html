Wild / Australian magpie

Australian magpie and swooping season

മാഗ്പൈ

Gymnorhina tibicen

A black-and-white bird with a beautiful song. Most of the year it is a friendly neighbour - but in spring a few magpies swoop on people who come near their nest.

Quick facts

Size36 to 44 cm long
FoodInsects and grubs it finds in the grass
WhereParks, farms, backyards and towns with trees and open grass
Swooping seasonMostly August to November, when there are chicks in the nest
Protected?Yes - under Queensland's Nature Conservation Act 1992

Why magpies swoop

A swooping magpie is a parent protecting its chicks. It sees you as a threat to the nest, so it flies low over your head, clacks its beak and sometimes hits you. Only a small number of magpies swoop, and they often pick the same kinds of people again, such as walkers or cyclists who pass the nest every day.

What to do in swooping season

Is it dangerous?

Most magpies never swoop. A swoop is a fright more than an injury, but a bird can hit your head, and cyclists can fall off when they panic.

  • Walk - do not run - and move away from the area quickly.
  • If you are on a bike, get off and walk it past the nest.
  • Wear a hat and sunglasses, or carry an umbrella.
  • Take a different route while the birds are nesting.
  • Never throw things at a magpie or touch its nest. It is against the law, and it makes the bird swoop more.
  • Keep an eye on children in places where magpies swoop.

Who to call: if a magpie is swooping in a park, street or school, tell your local council - they can put up warning signs. For advice, call the Queensland Government on 13 QGOV (13 74 68).

Questions

When is magpie swooping season in Queensland?

Mostly from August to November, when magpies have eggs and chicks. It can start in late winter and carry on into early summer.

Why do magpies swoop?

To protect their chicks. A swooping magpie sees you as a danger to its nest. Only a small number of magpies swoop.

Can I kill or move a swooping magpie?

No. Magpies are protected under Queensland's Nature Conservation Act 1992. Harming them or their nests is against the law, and a magpie can only be moved with a permit. Contact your local council or the Queensland Government instead.

What do magpies eat?

Mostly insects and grubs, which they find by walking on the grass and listening and probing for them.
